I am an applied machine learning researcher working on AI and computational methods for drug discovery. I joined Calici Co., Ltd. in 2021 as its first employee and have led the company's AI team since 2026. At Calici I have developed many of the AI models behind Pharmaco-Net, the company's AI-driven structure-based drug discovery platform, and I am a co-inventor on a granted US patent and further patent applications for AI- and GPU-based drug candidate discovery systems. I have also contributed to collaborative drug discovery projects with universities, research institutes, and pharmaceutical companies in Korea and abroad.

My research centres on predicting how molecules behave in the body: absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET). Data in this area are often small, noisy, and held by institutions that cannot share them, so I focus on models that remain reliable under these conditions and on evaluating their gains rigorously. This led to [CaliciBoost](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-025-01137-7) (*Journal of Cheminformatics*, 2025), a systematic study of molecular representations for Caco-2 permeability prediction that ranked first on the TDC Caco-2 leaderboard. My team and I also represent Calici in a national federated learning consortium for drug-induced liver injury (DILI) prediction. Alongside this, I work on structure-based design, including molecular dynamics and free-energy analysis of protein–ligand, protein–peptide, and antibody–antigen complexes. In a collaboration with The Ohio State University, I carried out the in silico analysis for a 2026 [*PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2614150123) study on hepatitis E virus replication, using docking and molecular dynamics to characterise how fatty acids bind the viral ORF1 protein.

My route into this field has been interdisciplinary. I hold a Master of International Business from La Trobe University, Australia (2013), and an MSc in Science and Technology Information Science from UST, Korea (2018), where my research on technology and innovation was published in [*Technological Forecasting and Social Change*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2018.06.021) (2019). This grounding in empirical research continues to shape my emphasis on data quality and rigorous evaluation.

**Research interests**
- ADMET and toxicity prediction under limited, heterogeneous data
- Federated and multitask learning for drug discovery
- Reliable evaluation of molecular property prediction models
- Structure-based design of small molecules, peptides, and antibodies
- Large-scale analysis of chemical and biological data

I am currently preparing to pursue doctoral research in AI for drug discovery, and I welcome conversations about collaboration.
