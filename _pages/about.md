I am an applied machine learning researcher working on AI and computational methods for drug discovery. In mid-2021 I joined Calici Co., Ltd. as its first employee. Calici is an AI drug discovery company in Daejeon whose core product, Pharmaco-Net, is an AI-driven structure-based platform for identifying new drug candidates. Working in the BIO-AI team, I developed and improved many of the AI models that power the platform. I have also contributed to collaborative drug discovery projects with universities, research institutes, and pharmaceutical companies in Korea and abroad, taking computational work from model building through to candidate design and analysis. Since the company reorganised its teams in early 2026, I have led its AI team.

My research centres on **predicting how molecules behave in the body**: absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET). Most of the data in this area is small, noisy, and scattered across institutions that cannot share it. I am interested in building models that remain useful under these conditions, and in measuring their gains honestly. This led to [CaliciBoost](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-025-01137-7) (*Journal of Cheminformatics*, 2025), a systematic study of molecular representations for Caco-2 permeability prediction that ranked first on the TDC Caco-2 leaderboard. My team and I currently represent Calici in a national federated learning consortium for drug-induced liver injury (DILI) prediction, where models are trained across pharmaceutical partners without pooling their data.

Alongside this, I work on structure-based design: molecular dynamics and free-energy analysis of protein–ligand, protein–peptide, and antibody–antigen complexes, peptide design, and ligand target prediction. In a collaboration with The Ohio State University, I carried out the in silico analysis for a 2026 *PNAS* study on hepatitis E virus (HEV) replication: docking fatty acids into the fatty acid–binding domain of the HEV ORF1 protein with Pharmaco-Net, characterising the protein–ligand interactions, running molecular dynamics simulations to examine binding in a dynamic setting, and analyzing the interaction between target protein with fatty acids. I am also a co-inventor on US patent applications for AI- and GPU-based drug candidate discovery systems.

My route into computational drug discovery has been interdisciplinary. I began in business and economics, completing a Master of International Business at La Trobe University, Australia (2013), and then moved into quantitative research with an MSc in Science and Technology Information Science at the University of Science and Technology (UST), Korea (2018). My master's research applied data-driven methods to the study of technology and innovation, and resulted in a first-author paper (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2018.06.021) in *Technological Forecasting and Social Change* (2019) analysing how the value propositions of internet start-ups have shifted from the customer's perspective; it has since been cited more than 60 times. This training in empirical methods and research design continues to inform my approach to machine learning in the life sciences, particularly my emphasis on data quality, rigorous evaluation, and the translational value of predictive models.

**Research interests**
- ADMET and toxicity prediction under limited, heterogeneous data
- Federated and multitask learning for drug discovery
- Reliable evaluation of molecular property prediction models
- Structure-based design of small molecules, peptides, and antibodies
- Large-scale analysis of chemical and biological data

I am currently preparing to pursue doctoral research in AI for drug discovery, and I welcome conversations about collaboration.
